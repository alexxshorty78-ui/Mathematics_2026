# Exercise 15. Classifying Three Systems

## Question

For each of the following systems, perform only as much elimination as necessary to recognize its type:

**System 1**

$$
\begin{cases}
x+y=2, \\
x-y=0, \\
\end{cases}
$$

**System 2**

$$
\begin{cases}
x+y=2, \\
2x+2y=4, \\
\end{cases}
$$

**System 3**

$$
\begin{cases}
x+y=2, \\
2x+2y=5. \\
\end{cases}
$$

For each, justify the classification: one solution, infinitely many solutions, or no solution.

## Solution

**System 1.** Subtract the second equation from the first to get $2y=2$. This determines $y=1$, and the first equation then determines $x=1$. The system has **one solution**.

**System 2.** Subtract twice the first equation from the second to get $0=0$. The first equation is the only constraint. With $y=s$, the solutions are $(2-s,s)$ for every $s\in\mathbb{R}$. The system has **infinitely many solutions**.

**System 3.** Subtract twice the first equation from the second to get $0=1$. This contradiction means the system has **no solution**.
