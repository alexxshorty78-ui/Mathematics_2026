# Exercise 4. Triangular Matrix

## Question

Without expanding the determinant, compute

$$
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 4 & 1 \\
0 & -3 & 5 \\
0 & 0 & 7 \\
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

State the rule for an arbitrary triangular matrix and explain it using this example.

## Solution

The given matrix is upper triangular. The determinant of a triangular matrix is the product of its diagonal entries. Thus,

$$
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 4 & 1 \\
0 & -3 & 5 \\
0 & 0 & 7 \\
\end{pmatrix}
=2\cdot(-3)\cdot7=-42.
$$

In general, for any triangular matrix, its determinant equals the product of its diagonal entries. Here the diagonal entries are $2$, $-3$, and $7$, so their product is $-42$.
